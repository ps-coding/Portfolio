Research

Both of my papers ask the same question from different directions: how well do AI systems work on noisy, real-world data?

AI-assisted discovery of physics laws

Summer 2025

NJ Governor's School in the Sciences at Drew University, advised by Dr. Minjoon Kouh · Paper (PDF) · Experiment materials (GitHub)

In 2020, Udrescu and Tegmark introduced AI Feynman, a system that rediscovered 100 equations from The Feynman Lectures on Physics using only tables of numerical data. One of its key techniques is symmetry detection: if a force depends only on the difference x0 − x1 between two positions, for example, those two variables can be combined into one, which reduces the dimensionality of the problem.

In the summer of 2025, at the New Jersey Governor's School in the Sciences, I led an eleven-person team that reimplemented the core of AI Feynman from scratch and tested it on more realistic data, with added noise and varying numerical ranges. I was the first author of the resulting IEEE paper.

We found that the system's accuracy degrades as noise increases and that it degrades faster for more complex equations. Across our test equations, the fit fell from R² ≈ 1.00 with no noise to approximately 0.49 at σ = 0.2 noise and below zero at σ = 0.4. However, the results varied considerably by equation: the simple kinematics equation vf = vi + at was still recovered almost exactly at a noise level of σ = 0.4 (R² = 0.99), whereas the standard oscillation-period equation T = 2π/ω collapsed at σ = 0.2. In one case, at a high noise level, the system overfit the equation U = mgh with an 18-term expression containing trigonometric functions.

My main contribution was a novel adaptive-α rule for symmetry detection. To test for a symmetry, the system shifts two variables by a constant α and uses a neural network trained on the data to check whether the output changes. A fixed α works well only for data of a particular scale: for values between −1 and 1, the shift is too large for the network to extrapolate accurately, and for values between −100 and 100, it is too small to change the output noticeably. My rule instead scales α based on the joint distributions of the columns being tested.

Across all of our test equations and data ranges, the adaptive rule detected nearly twice as many true symmetries (110 compared to 60) and reduced false positives tenfold (3 compared to 29), with the largest improvements on small numerical ranges.

The poster based on this paper won Best Poster at the IEEE MIT Undergraduate Research Technology Conference, placing first out of approximately 700 submissions.

Noise resistance of multimodal LLMs

2024–2025

Independent research · preprint (534 views, 242 downloads, and 5 citations on Google Scholar as of November 2026) · Paper (PDF) · DOI · Experiment materials (GitHub)

I conducted this study independently while I was in high school. At the time, the noise resistance of classification models and text-only LLMs had been investigated, but the noise resistance of multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) had not. To investigate it, I wrote 10 textual prompts and 30 image-based prompts and created a noisy version of each, using misspellings for the text and Gaussian, salt-and-pepper, or speckle noise for the images. I then created a denoised version of each noisy prompt with standard tools: the aspell spell-checker for text and OpenCV's Fast NL Means algorithm for images. I tested all 120 prompts on two MLLMs, GPT-4o and a locally hosted LLaVA model, and on GPT-3.5 as a text-only baseline, and I scored each of the 270 responses against rubrics that I had written in advance.

My first hypothesis, that MLLMs would be less resistant to noise than a text-only LLM (a hypothesis I developed based on the similar established result for classification models), was supported: textual noise significantly reduced the MLLMs' scores (p = 0.025 for the two combined) but did not change GPT-3.5's, and the smaller MLLM, LLaVA, was affected more than GPT-4o (p = 0.048 for LLaVA alone). My second hypothesis, that denoising would improve performance, was refuted, which I found to be the most interesting result of the study: spell-checking the prompts significantly reduced the MLLMs' scores (p = 0.010), and denoising the images did not improve them either.

One plausible explanation is that LLMs, having been trained on vast quantities of text, are already more capable spell-checkers than aspell, so pre-processing a prompt with a less capable tool introduces more errors than it removes. Because this was still a relatively small study due to the resources I had available at the time, the results should be confirmed with larger sample sizes, and future work could also test a small LLM as the denoiser.

JainClock

2026

Sole developer · Embedded (hardware) project · GitHub

JainClock is an Arduino-powered, 3D-printed clock with features designed for Jains. Many Jain practices are timed by the sun and the lunar calendar: the Navkarshi vow, for example, ends 48 minutes after sunrise, and the Chauvihar vow prohibits food and water from sunset to sunrise. The clock therefore displays the local sunrise and sunset times and the current tithi (lunar day) alongside the time, and it includes a reference to 16 pachkhans (vows) with their timings and instructions.

The clock runs on an Arduino GIGA R1 with a GIGA Display Shield, and I wrote its firmware in C++ with the LVGL graphics library. I managed TCP connections established via the board's WiFi chip to retrieve live data from a small Express server that I also wrote, which combines geolocation, time, astronomy, and tithi APIs into a single response so that the microcontroller only needs to make one request.

C++, Arduino, LVGL, TCP, PlatformIO, Node.js, Express

SocialScanner

2025

Team lead · GitHub

SocialScanner is a privacy-first, machine-learning-based mental-health screening system that a partner and I developed to help school counselors identify early warning signs in students, ultimately aiming to help more students get the help they need and deserve. It analyzes research-backed metrics for mental health, including grade trends, social media text and images, and audio recordings or transcripts of counselor sessions. After we presented it to the Northern Burlington County Regional High School administration, the school gave the project its commendation and backing. To ensure safety, all features have explicit guards, unremovable by schools or even us, to ensure student consent (e.g., for social media data, the student must explicitly approve a request from Instagram in order to have their data monitored—this is a decision students/families can make for themselves).

To provide more optionality, ease adoption, and protect student privacy, we used a one-model-per-metric architecture, which allows a school to activate only the signals it chooses. We built the models in PyTorch and used parallel computing to generate counselor reports in bulk. We also presented the project at a national TSA conference.

PyTorch, Computer Vision, NLP, Web Scraping, Parallel Computing, Python

Ultra Ball

2024

Sole developer · Play it · GitHub

Ultra Ball is a fun, quite overengineered game that I play with my family friends. It began in 2018 as a hand game that my closest friend group invented by extending War. Our parents are first-generation immigrants from India who met in college, so we have always had a close bond, and the game started as a simple pastime for us. As in Rock, Paper, Scissors, every player chooses a move at the same time, but Ultra Ball has 15+ moves instead of three (reloads, offensive moves, defensive moves, and moves that are both), which makes it a mix of strategy, luck, and psychology.

I developed the online version so that anyone could play it, alone against bots or with friends. All of the game logic runs on a fully custom WebSocket server that any client can connect to: it resolves the interactions between the moves, automates the lobby lifecycle, and manages the bot opponents, each of which combines a behavior tree with a randomized personality. The client is built with SvelteKit and TypeScript. The game grew organically to more than 500 daily active users within months of its launch.

WebSockets, SvelteKit, Behavior-tree AI, TypeScript
